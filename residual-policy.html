Residual Policy
Learning.

Can a small learned correction, bounded and safety-shielded, fix where a frozen robot policy fails? I trained a behavior-cloning backbone on a simulated grasp-and-lift task, traced exactly where and why it fails, and designed a bounded residual policy and safety shield around it.

Problem

A common way to deploy learned robot policies is a frozen pretrained backbone (for example a Vision-Language-Action model), a small residual policy fine-tuned on task data to correct per-deployment errors, and a safety shield that bounds whatever action actually reaches the robot.

I built a miniature version of that stack on robomimic's Lift task: a simulated Panda arm has to grasp a cube and lift it. The backbone is a small behavior-cloning (BC) network that I train and then freeze. The questions: where exactly does the backbone fail, and can a correction that is only allowed to make small moves fix it, without ever sending the robot an unsafe command?

The task

Approach

1. A frozen BC backbone

A 19→256→256→7 MLP with ReLU and a tanh output, so it can never emit actions outside the controller's [−1, 1] range, trained with MSE on normalized observations. To pick the training budget I held out 10% of demonstrations (not individual transitions, since consecutive transitions are near-duplicates and would leak). Validation error bottomed out around epoch 8, and the later rise came almost entirely from the gripper: longer training memorized each demo's gripper timing without improving the arm. I fixed the budget at 15 epochs, trained on all 200 demos, then froze the network and asserted it stayed frozen.

2. Finding where BC fails

Offline, translation error is largest early in each demo, and gripper error is nearly zero except in the grasp phase. The gripper sign disagrees with the demonstrator on only 2.14% of steps, but 99% of those disagreements fall within ±5 steps of the demonstrator's own close event. Gripper errors are timing errors at the grasp, not wrong decisions elsewhere.

Online, I instrumented 30 rollouts and classified every failure:

Failed rollouts still get within about 3 cm of the cube (6 never quite reached it, 2 closed but missed or slipped). So the failures aren't gross reaching errors. They're a precision problem in the final approach: BC stalls a few centimeters off, closes late, toggles the gripper, and loops. That is the classic compounding error of imitation learning once the state drifts off the demonstrations. It sets the design goal for the residual: small, translation-dominated corrections, conditioned on what BC is about to do.

3. A bounded residual policy

a = clip( aBC(s) + δ, −1, 1 )
δ = bound ⊙ tanh( f([ s, aBC(s) ]) )
f is a 2×128 MLP whose last layer starts at zero, so training begins exactly at BC.
  • Conditioned on BC's action, so the head can learn "BC is about to undershoot here" directly instead of re-deriving BC's output from the state.
  • Bounds from the data: each action dimension's bound is the 90th percentile of BC's own error on that dimension. A single fixed bound like 0.05 would be too small to fix translation errors yet larger than the demonstrators' entire spread on rotation. The gripper bound is deliberately too small to flip open/close on its own; it can only nudge timing.
  • TD3+BC (twin critics, delayed actor, a behavior-cloning term) keeps the correction on the support of the expert data, which matters when every demonstration is expert.
  • No reward shaping. A distance-to-cube bonus isn't potential-based, so it changes the optimal policy and rewards hovering near the cube, which is exactly BC's failure mode.
  • Clipped target actions: the critic's target uses the action the robot would actually execute, so it never bootstraps from impossible actions the actor could exploit.
  • Training: soft target updates (τ = 0.005), 5,000 steps at batch 256. Critic loss fell from 0.21 to about 3×10−4 and both losses were flat after about 3,000 steps.

4. A safety shield

The last line of defense clips each action dimension to the range the demonstrators used, widened by exactly that dimension's residual bound (the largest legitimate correction), replaces NaN/Inf with a no-op, and counts clips per dimension. I used per-dimension bounds instead of one L2 ball because the dimensions have very different units (translation, rotation, and a binary gripper), and per-axis limits map directly onto real hardware limits.

What broke, and how I'm fixing it

The install step failed silently

Symptom
The environment setup printed "Installed." even though a dependency had failed to build.
Cause
robomimic's egl_probe dependency doesn't build on current Colab, and pip installs all-or-nothing, so robomimic was never installed.
Fix
Installed robosuite and MuJoCo normally and robomimic with --no-deps; egl_probe is only needed by a wrapper this project never calls.

Two training diagnostics were stuck at zero

Symptom
The Q-value and residual-magnitude logs read 0.0 for the whole run, while the losses moved normally.
Cause
Those two prints were still placeholders, so the run never recorded the two signals that would confirm the critic is honest and the residual stays small.
Fix
Two lines: log min(Q1, Q2).mean() and the mean |δ| each print.

The shield blocked the gripper from closing

Symptom
With the shield on, the first full evaluation scored 0/30, even though the residual is bounded to small corrections and trained normally.
Cause
The shield's upper bound was computed from the demonstrations' minimum instead of their maximum, squeezing every action into a thin band near the bottom of its range. On the gripper that caps the command near fully open, so the robot can never grasp.
Fix
One line: build the upper bound from the demo maximum. The corrected evaluation is the next run.

What I learned

  • Diagnose before you design. Classifying every failure showed the problem was the last 3 cm of approach and the grasp timing, which shaped every residual decision.
  • Derive limits from the data. Per-dimension bounds from BC's actual errors and the demonstrators' actual range beat any single hand-picked number.
  • Question your tools. A setup step reporting success, logs reading zero, and a safety layer that silently blocks the task are all failures that look like results. Each needed a check against a known answer.

What I'd do next

  • Re-run the evaluation with the corrected shield and live Q / residual-magnitude diagnostics, and check Q against Monte-Carlo returns.
  • Ablate the design: TD3+BC vs. pure distillation, and clipped vs. unclipped target actions.
  • Target the gripper directly with a classification head or a gripper-specific residual.
  • Evaluate on 100+ rollouts: at 30, one rollout is 3.3 points and the binomial standard error is about 8 points.
